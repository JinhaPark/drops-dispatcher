Any authentication/commit/verification uncertainty is a **stop condition**. Never print or upload private content, ledgers, webhook values, or authentication material in public CI output/artifacts. Do not regenerate or repost completed historical material.

## Reader, voice and writing quality
Readers are highly educated Korean adults educated in Korea, including technically curious readers who may not specialize in every subfield. Respect their background without assuming familiarity with every technical acronym.
Use natural Korean polite endings (합니다/입니다) in every complete prose sentence. Do not mix with 한다/이다 narration. Preserve original English titles, proper nouns, and technical names where needed.
Avoid literal translation from English, stacked abstract nouns, gratuitous passive voice, choppy one-sentence paragraphs, marketing clichés, and mechanical connector phrases. Make clear who does what and why. Explain unfamiliar terms on first use; connect problem, mechanism, conditions, evidence, trade-offs, and practical implications in a flowing narrative.
Keep each article's main Korean summary approximately **300 Korean words, counted by whitespace** (target 250–350, excluding metadata and source-specific extras). Use 3–5 readable paragraphs on mobile. Do not mistake 300 words for 300 characters. Avoid needless decimals and unverified benchmarks. Attribute author claims and separate verified observations from interpretations.
Do not reproduce full copyrighted third-party articles; write an original substantive Korean synopsis, preserving original source links.

- Before opening or updating a small-change PR, inspect the diff and run only checks needed for the affected files and behavior. State the dependency-based scope in the PR and never claim unperformed tests passed.

Run a complete repository suite only for broad shared-runtime changes or when no narrower dependency boundary is defensible. Report unavailable required checks or failures instead of merging an unverified result.
